Waste Management Guide

Sorting waste at the source is the single easiest habit that makes recycling and composting possible. Learn what goes where.

Wet waste

Examples

Food scrapsFruit and vegetable peelsTea leavesGarden leaves

How to dispose

Use the wet-waste bin. Keep plastic wrappers and tissues out, and empty the bin daily.

Can be composted into soil nutrients and keeps food waste out of landfills.

Dry waste

Examples

PaperCardboardClean wrappersCloth scraps

How to dispose

Keep it clean and dry. Flatten boxes and put everything in the dry-waste bin.

Clean, dry waste is far easier to sort and send for recycling.

E-waste

Examples

Chargers and cablesBatteriesOld phonesEarphones

How to dispose

Never put it in a normal bin. Hand it to a department e-waste collection point or an authorised recycler.

Recovers useful metals and keeps harmful substances out of soil and water.

Recyclable materials

Examples

Plastic bottlesGlass bottlesMetal cansClean paper

How to dispose

Empty, rinse and dry each item, then place it in the recycling bin.

Saves raw materials and the energy needed to make new products.

Save Water and Electricity

Most savings come from small habits that take seconds. Pick one tip from each group and make it automatic.

Water-saving tips

Close taps properly

Turn the tap off fully after every use. A slow drip adds up over a whole day.

Reminder: check the tap before you walk away.

Report water leaks

Tell the maintenance office about leaking taps, pipes and flush tanks as soon as you see them.

Reminder: a quick report saves a lot of water.

Avoid unnecessary usage

Use only what you need: fill your bottle to the amount you will drink and switch the tap off while washing hands.

Reminder: pour only what you will finish.

Reuse water where appropriate

Use leftover drinking water for plants. Use non-drinking water for cleaning jobs where it is safe to do so.

Reminder: never reuse water for drinking.

Electricity-saving tips

Switch off unused lights and fans

The last person to leave a classroom, lab or hostel room switches everything off.

Reminder: look up before you leave.

Use energy-efficient lighting

LED lights give the same brightness for much less power and last longer than older bulbs.

Reminder: suggest LEDs when a bulb needs replacing.

Shut down unused computers

Shut computers and monitors down at the end of a lab session instead of leaving them on standby.

Reminder: shut down, then unplug if you can.

Make better use of daylight

Open curtains and blinds, and sit near windows so lights are not needed during the day.

Reminder: daylight is free and always on.

Eco Challenges

Five campus challenges you can run with your class, hostel or eco club. These are information cards: they do not register or track anyone. Speak to your faculty coordinator to organise one.

Plastic-Free Campus Day

Spend a full day without single-use plastic: no plastic cups, straws, carry bags or packaged snacks.

Suggested duration: 1 day

Cuts plastic waste that would otherwise end up in bins and drains.

Challenge details
  • Carry a cloth bag and a steel or glass bottle.
  • Ask the canteen to use steel plates and reusable cups for the day.
  • Count the plastic items you avoided and share the number.

Plant-a-Tree Challenge

Plant a native sapling on campus and take responsibility for watering it as it settles in.

Suggested duration: 1 day to plant, then regular care

Trees give shade, support birds and insects, and absorb carbon dioxide as they grow.

Challenge details
  • Get permission from the college and choose a suitable spot.
  • Pick native species that suit your local climate.
  • Make a small watering rota among friends.

Bring-Your-Own-Bottle Challenge

Carry a reusable bottle every day and refill it at water points instead of buying bottled water.

Suggested duration: 2 weeks

Every refill replaces a bottle that would have been thrown away.

Challenge details
  • Wash your bottle regularly.
  • Note on a calendar each day you remember it.
  • Keep a spare bottle in your bag or hostel room.

Campus Clean-Up Drive

Team up to collect litter from grounds, corridors and the area outside the gate, and sort it as you go.

Suggested duration: 2 to 3 hours

A cleaner campus is safer, healthier and nicer to study in.

Challenge details
  • Wear gloves and closed shoes. Never pick up broken glass or sharp items by hand.
  • Carry separate bags for dry waste and recyclables.
  • Hand the bags to the college housekeeping team.

Save Electricity Week

For seven days, make sure every light, fan, projector and computer is off when nobody is using it.

Suggested duration: 7 days

Less electricity used means lower bills and fewer emissions from power generation.

Challenge details
  • Appoint a "switch monitor" for each room.
  • Stick reminder notes next to switches.
  • Compare the electricity meter reading before and after, if your college allows.

Recycling Information

Recycling only works when the right materials arrive clean and sorted. Here is the short version.

Why recycling matters

Recycling turns used materials into new products, so fewer raw materials are mined, cut or drilled. It also reduces the amount of waste sent to landfills and often uses less energy than making things from scratch.

What can usually be recycled

  • Paper and cardboard
  • Plastic bottles and containers
  • Glass bottles and jars
  • Metal cans and tins

Local rules differ, so check what your college's collector accepts.

How to prepare materials

  1. Empty the container completely.
  2. Rinse off food and drink.
  3. Let it dry.
  4. Flatten boxes and crush bottles if allowed.
  5. Place it in the recycling bin, not the general bin.

Reduce single-use plastics

  • Carry a reusable bottle and cloth bag
  • Bring your own cup and cutlery
  • Say no to straws and extra packaging
  • Choose refills and bulk options

A cleaner campus

When waste is sorted properly, bins stay cleaner, housekeeping teams work faster, and less litter ends up on lawns and drains. Recycling also gives the campus a visible shared goal: every sorted bottle is a small, countable win.

About EcoTrack

EcoTrack is a college mini project that shows how a campus can make sustainability simple, visible and shared.

Our mission

To help every student understand the small daily choices that reduce waste and save resources, and to make those choices easy to start.

Environmental goals

  • Segregate waste at the source
  • Use less water and electricity
  • Recycle more and use less single-use plastic
  • Grow the campus tree cover

Benefits for students

Practical habits, leadership experience through challenges, and a healthier place to study and live.

Benefits for the college

Lower utility bills, less waste to manage, and a stronger green reputation.

How you can contribute

  • Sort your waste every day
  • Switch off lights, fans and taps
  • Report leaks and faults
  • Run or join an eco challenge
  • Share these tips with a friend
